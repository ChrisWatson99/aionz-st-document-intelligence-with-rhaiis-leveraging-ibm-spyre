Potential SysProg Concerns for the Stated Requirements

This document captures, without summarization, the concerns that a z/OS SysProg or Linux on Z administrator might raise when reviewing the following requirements:

  • Secure Service Container (SSC) LPARs: One for ACC and two for SSA
  • Appliance Control Center (ACC)
  • Spyre Support Appliance (SSA)
  • Ansible Playbooks
  • Linux on IBM Z LPAR
  • RHEL 9.6
  • Podman
  • Git
  • User logged in with sudo access
  • Hugging Face account, access token, huggingface-cli, and pre-populated models
  • Access to registry.redhat.io

1. Why are three Secure Service Container (SSC) LPARs required?

Requirement:

  • 1 ACC LPAR
  • 2 SSA LPARs

Typical SysProg questions:

  • Why three dedicated SSCs?
  • Can ACC and SSA coexist?
  • What are the CPU, memory, DASD, and network requirements?
  • What is the high-availability story?
  • What is the backup/recovery story?
  • What is the failure impact if one SSA is lost?

SSC environments are intentionally restrictive. Many customers are cautious about proliferating SSC LPARs because they consume processor and memory resources, create additional operational objects to manage, may behave more like appliances than traditional Linux systems, and often require special operational procedures.

A SysProg may ask: “Are these truly production requirements, or are they merely deployment recommendations?”

2. Security and Compliance Concerns

Hugging Face Account

Requirement: You have a Hugging Face account and access token.

Questions:

  • Is outbound internet access required?
  • Can models be downloaded from the internet?
  • Are customer prompts or data ever sent externally?
  • How are tokens stored?
  • Is a personal account acceptable or is an enterprise account required?

Large enterprises frequently prohibit personal cloud accounts, downloading arbitrary models, and direct internet connectivity from production systems.

A security officer may immediately ask: “Why are we introducing another external identity provider?”

Root/Sudo Access

Requirement: Logged in as a user with sudo access.

  • Who is permitted sudo?
  • Does this violate least-privilege policies?
  • Can deployment be automated without permanent sudo?

Many production Linux on Z systems are tightly controlled and prefer role-based access or temporary privilege elevation.

Container Images

Requirements include Podman and registry.redhat.io access.

  • Which container images?
  • Are they signed?
  • How are vulnerabilities tracked?
  • How frequently are images updated?

Security teams increasingly require image scanning, CVE reporting, and SBOM documentation before production approval.

3. Capacity Planning Concerns

The biggest question a SysProg will ask is: “What models?”

The requirement stating that models are pre-populated in ~/models/ immediately raises questions.

  • 3B model?
  • 8B model?
  • 70B model?
  • Granite?
  • Llama?
  • Mixtral?

The hardware requirements differ dramatically.

  • Huge pages
  • CPU consumption
  • Vector execution utilization

4. Linux on Z Resource Consumption

Requirements include RHEL 9.6 and Podman.

  • Dedicated LPAR?
  • Shared LPAR?
  • z/VM guest?
  • Number of CPUs?
  • Memory sizing?

AI applications frequently request far more memory than traditional middleware workloads.

5. Networking Concerns

ACC, SSA, Linux LPARs, Git, Hugging Face, and Red Hat registries all imply network flows.

  • Which ports are needed?
  • Are firewalls involved?
  • Is internet access mandatory?
  • Can a proxy be used?
  • Is TLS inspection supported?

Many z/OS environments are intentionally isolated. A common response is: “We don't allow production systems to reach the public internet.”

This commonly leads to discussions about private registries, mirrored repositories, and offline model loading.

6. Change Management Concerns

Ansible sounds attractive from an engineering perspective but raises governance questions.

  • Are playbooks idempotent?
  • Who owns them?
  • Are they vendor supplied?
  • Are they customer modifiable?
  • Do they require root?

A SysProg is often responsible for ensuring repeatability, auditability, and rollback capability.

8. Disaster Recovery Concerns

The requirement list says nothing about:

  • Backup
  • Restore
  • Disaster recovery
  • Clustering

Questions include:

  • What happens if ACC is lost?
  • Can SSA be rebuilt automatically?
  • How are models restored?
  • Are Git repositories part of DR?
  • Are SSC images backed up?

9. Operational Monitoring Concerns

  • What monitoring is required?
  • What logs must be retained?
  • What alerts are generated?
  • How are container failures detected?

Typical requirements include integration with Syslog, Splunk, Elastic, or ServiceNow.

10. Software Lifecycle Concerns

  • When RHEL 9.7 arrives, what happens?
  • What Podman versions are supported?
  • How are AI models updated?
  • How often are containers rebuilt?
  • How are security patches applied?

Appliance-style deployments can become problematic when lifecycle ownership is unclear.

11. Data Governance Concerns

The phrase pre-populated ~/models/ commonly triggers governance reviews.

  • Where did the model originate?
  • What license applies?
  • Can it be used commercially?
  • Can customer data be processed?
  • Does the model contain restricted content?

These concerns are especially common in banking, insurance, healthcare, and government environments.

12. z/OS-Specific Concerns

A z/OS SysProg may ask: “What benefit am I getting for three more LPARs and a Linux stack?”

From the z/OS perspective, this infrastructure introduces additional complexity:

  • More HMC definitions
  • More network definitions
  • More RACF administration
  • More capacity planning
  • More backup requirements
  • More patching windows
  • More audits

The business case must clearly justify this operational burden.

Most Likely Objections

  1. Why are three SSC LPARs required?
  2. What are the exact CPU, memory, and DASD requirements?
  3. Why is external Hugging Face access needed?
  4. Can deployment be performed in an air-gapped environment?
  5. Who owns and supports each component?
  6. What is the DR and recovery strategy?
  7. What data leaves the enterprise network?
  8. How are AI models licensed, patched, and governed?
  9. What monitoring and operational tooling is required?
  10. What is the ongoing operational cost in SysProg effort?

For many IBM Z customers, concerns about external connectivity, air-gapped deployment, and operational support ownership are among the most common reasons that solution approval slows or stalls.
